GST (Goods and Services Tax) Quick Reference Notes — India

1. OVERVIEW
GST is a single indirect tax on the supply of goods and services. It replaced
multiple cascading taxes like VAT, excise, and service tax under one unified
framework. GST was introduced on 1 July 2017 and is governed by the GST Council.

2. GST RATES
The four main GST rate slabs are: 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%.
- 5%: essential items — food grains, packaged food, footwear below Rs 500,
  transport services.
- 12%: processed food, business class air tickets, cell phones, fertilisers.
- 18%: most services (IT, consulting, restaurant dining), capital goods, industrial
  intermediates, electronic goods, white goods.
- 28%: luxury goods — automobiles, tobacco, aerated drinks, cement, ACs.
Some goods are GST-exempt (0%): fresh fruits, vegetables, milk, bread, healthcare
services, educational services.

3. GST COMPONENTS
- CGST (Central GST): levied by the central government on intra-state supply.
- SGST (State GST): levied by the state government on intra-state supply.
- IGST (Integrated GST): levied on inter-state supply; equal to CGST + SGST.
For an intra-state supply taxed at 18%, the buyer pays 9% CGST + 9% SGST.
For an inter-state supply at 18%, the buyer pays 18% IGST.

4. INPUT TAX CREDIT (ITC)
Businesses can claim credit for GST paid on inputs against their output GST
liability. Example: if a manufacturer pays 18% GST on raw materials and collects
18% on finished goods, the input tax is deducted. ITC prevents cascading (tax on
tax) and reduces the effective cost.

5. GSTIN (GST Identification Number)
Every registered taxpayer has a 15-character GSTIN:
- First 2 digits: state code (e.g. 27 = Maharashtra, 29 = Karnataka).
- Next 10 characters: PAN of the entity.
- 13th character: entity number within the PAN.
- 14th character: Z (default).
- 15th character: check digit.
Example: 27AABCA1234A1Z5.

6. HSN AND SAC CODES
- HSN (Harmonized System of Nomenclature): classifies goods. Example: 7318 = bolts
  and screws; 8471 = computers; 9405 = lighting.
- SAC (Services Accounting Code): classifies services. Example: 998311 = management
  consulting; 997212 = air transport of passengers.
Businesses with turnover above Rs 5 crore must report 6-digit HSN codes on invoices.

7. E-INVOICING
E-invoicing is mandatory for businesses with turnover above Rs 5 crore (as of
August 2023). The seller generates a JSON invoice, uploads it to the Invoice
Registration Portal (IRP), and receives a signed IRN and QR code. This ensures
real-time validation and reduces fake invoicing.

8. GST RETURNS
- GSTR-1: outward supply details (filed monthly/quarterly).
- GSTR-3B: summary return with tax payment (filed monthly).
- GSTR-9: annual return consolidating the year's data.
Late filing attracts a penalty of Rs 50/day (Rs 20 for nil returns).
